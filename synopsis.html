1. Introduction

Brain tumors represent one of the most significant neurological challenges in modern medicine. Early and accurate detection is paramount to improving survival rates and tailoring effective treatment strategies. Traditionally, the identification of tumorous cells in Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) scans has been the exclusive domain of senior radiologists—a process that is time-intensive and subject to intra-observer variability.

TumorAi is an innovative mobile application engineered to bridge the gap between complex neuroimaging and real-time accessibility. By leveraging On-Device Machine Learning (ODML) and Mobile Vision techniques, the system enables medical practitioners to perform rapid preliminary screenings directly from their mobile devices, ensuring that critical diagnostic insights are available at the point of care.

2. Problem Statement

The diagnosis of brain tumors suffers from several bottlenecks in the current healthcare landscape:

  • Specialist Scarcity: In many rural and underserved regions, access to specialists who can accurately interpret MRI scans is limited.
  • Latency: The turnaround time for traditional radiology reports can delay critical surgical or therapeutic interventions.
  • Human Error: Fatigue and high workload can lead to oversight of subtle malignant indicators in complex neuro-imaging datasets.

There is a compelling need for a standardized, automated tool that can provide a second opinion or a primary filter to prioritize urgent cases.

3. Literature Review

Extensive research into Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) has demonstrated their superior efficacy in medical image classification Task compared to traditional feature extraction methods. Models like VGG16, ResNet, and MobileNetV2 have been benchmarked for tumor detection with accuracies exceeding 95% in controlled experimental settings.

Historically, such models required high-performance GPUs for execution. However, the advent of TensorFlow Lite (TFLite) and the Android Neural Networks API (NNAPI) has facilitated a paradigm shift toward edge computing. TumorAi builds upon this foundation by integrating a weight-quantized CNN model tailored for the constrained computational environment of a smartphone, without significantly compromising diagnostic precision.

4. Objectives

The primary goals of this project are:

  • Mobile Integration: To develop a robust Android application that provides a seamless workflow from scan selection to result visualization.
  • Real-time Inference: To minimize diagnostic latency by performing all ML computations locally on the device's CPU/GPU.
  • Cloud Synchronization: To implement securely managed cloud storage for scan history, allowing longitudinal tracking of patient data.
  • User-Centric Design: To provide an intuitive interface that displays both the binary classification ("Tumor Detected" vs. "No Tumor") and a confidence percentage to assist clinical decision-making.

5. Methodology

The system employs a multi-stage pipeline designed for reliability and speed:

Step 1: Data Acquisition & Preprocessing
MRI scans are imported via the Android system gallery. The images are dynamically resized to 224x224 pixels and normalized to a [0, 1] range to match the input specifications of the pre-trained neural network.

Step 2: On-Device Inference
The project utilizes a `TFLiteHelper` class that manages an `Interpreter` instance. The pre-processed image buffer is fed into the TFLite model (`brain_tumor_model.tflite`), which outputs a probability array representing the likelihood of tumor presence.

Step 3: Post-processing & Visualization
The raw probabilities are converted into human-readable results. A "Thresholding" logic determines the final diagnosis (e.g., > 0.5 probability indicates tumor detection).

Step 4: Secure Data Retention
Results, confidence scores, and timestamps are automatically synchronized with Firebase Firestore, building a historical log for each user.

7. Expected Outcomes

The successful implementation of TumorAi is expected to yield:

  • An 80-90% reduction in the initial screening time for brain MRI scans.
  • A high-fidelity classification system that serves as a reliable decision-support tool.
  • A centralized, cloud-synced database that enables healthcare workers to monitor scan trends over time.

8. Applications

The utility of this project extends beyond general diagnostics:

  • Clinical Assistance: Acts as a preliminary validation tool for junior doctors and residents.
  • Rural Tele-Medicine: Provides a "smart" screening layer in regions with no resident radiologist.
  • Academic Use: Serves as a reference implementation for students studying the intersection of ML and Healthcare.

9. Conclusion and Future Work

TumorAi demonstrates the profound potential of mobile-first AI in revolutionizing medical diagnostics. By decentralizing the computational burden from a central server to the smartphone in a doctor’s pocket, we move closer to democratizing high-quality healthcare.

Future Work: Future iterations will focus on multi-class classification to distinguish between different tumor types (e.g., Glioma, Meningioma, Pituitary). Additionally, we aim to implement Heatmap visualization (Grad-CAM) to highlight the specific regions of the brain triggering the 'Positive' diagnosis, thereby enhancing the interpretability of our model for surgical planning.
